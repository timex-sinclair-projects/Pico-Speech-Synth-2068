ZX Voice Rev B Hardware

The board that replaces the 2023 RP2040-Zero card: same ZX Voice port map, all eight data bits shifted, strict port decode, an I2S amplifier, the RP2040 on the board, and a TS1000 edge that also fits the 2068. Every 5 V signal crosses to the Pico through a 74LVC245.

Design in one paragraph

A two-layer card on the TS1000 edge footprint. A 74HCT688 compares A0–A3, A6, A7 against solder jumpers and a 74HCT138 splits A4/A5 and /RD into four strobes, so the board answers exactly to 0x17 (OUT 23), 0x27 (IN 39), 0x37 (OUT 55 / IN 55) unless a "loose decode" jumper restores the original three-bit decode. Three 74LVC245s carry D0–D7 in, the strobes plus /RESET and CLK in, and a status byte out; a 74LVC1G125 drives D7 during IN 39. Reads are answered by hardware, so no firmware timing is involved. The RP2040 sits on the board with a 4 MB flash, a 12 MHz crystal, a 3.3 V LDO and USB-C. Audio is a MAX98357A I2S amplifier into a 4–8 Ω speaker, with a PWM line-out kept for headphones. Power is the bus 5 V or USB, diode-ORed, with a polyfuse on the bus side.

03Port decode

  • Why HCT. The 2068's /IORQ, /RD and /M1 reach the rear connector through 74LS buffers whose guaranteed high is 2.7 V. HC inputs at 5 V want 3.5 V; HCT inputs switch at 1.4 V. The 2023 board's HC138 works by grace of typical levels.
  • Why not /WR. The '138 uses /RD as in the original: a write cycle is /IORQ low with /RD high. /M1 on G1 keeps interrupt-acknowledge cycles (both /IORQ and /M1 low) out.
  • Timing. '688 (typ. 25 ns) + '138 (typ. 20 ns) + '245 (typ. 5 ns) puts /ALD at the Pico about 50 ns after /IORQ falls, inside a cycle of roughly 700 ns at 3.25–3.5 MHz. The Z80 has had data on the bus since T1.
  • Port 0x07. The strict decode's fourth slot is never enabled by this board. On the ZX81 it is ZXpand's port, so leave it that way.

04Reads answered in hardware

The status byte on IN 55 is a firmware convention, not a chip feature: bit 7 ready (same as IN 39), bit 6 text buffer has room, bit 5 talking, bit 4 engine (0 NRL, 1 CTS256), bits 0–3 reserved. It costs eight GPIOs and one '245, both of which the bare RP2040 has to spare; on a hand-built RP2040-Zero card the part is simply not fitted.

  • All four run from 3.3 V. LVC inputs tolerate 5.5 V even with VCC absent, which covers the half millisecond after power-on when the Z80 bus is already live and the LDO is still ramping. That is the reason the RP2350's own 5 V tolerance would not have removed these parts.
  • A 3.3 V high from U_R and U_B is a legal TTL high (Z80 VIH is 2.0 V). 33 Ω series resistors on the eight U_R outputs and on the '1G125 output tame ringing on the unterminated bus.
  • Ground: one full plane, both GND fingers, and a 100 nF decoupler per logic part next to its VCC pin. The retro-comp reports of flaky '245 boards trace to single ground returns and unmatched ground bounce, not to the parts.

06RP2040 minimal circuit

Straight from Raspberry Pi's "Hardware design with RP2040" minimal example, which is what the assembler's part library is built around.

  • Flash: W25Q32JVSSIQ, 4 MB, SOIC-8, on QSPI with 22–33 Ω series on CLK. BOOTSEL button pulls QSPI_SS (CS) low through 1 kΩ.
  • Clock: ABM8-272-T3 12 MHz crystal, 15 pF load capacitors, 1 kΩ in series with XOUT.
  • Power: AP2112K-3.3 LDO (600 mA, SOT-23-5) from the board 5 V rail; 1 µF on each of the two DVDD (1.1 V internal regulator) pins; 100 nF on every IOVDD/DVDD pin plus a 10 µF bulk on 3.3 V. VREG_IN and VREG_VOUT wired as in the reference design.
  • USB: USB-C receptacle (16-pin, USB 2.0 only), 5.1 kΩ from each CC pin to ground so a host will supply 5 V, 27 Ω series on D+ and D−, ESD diode array optional. VBUS goes to the power OR, never straight to the bus 5 V.
  • RUN: 10 kΩ pull-up and a reset button. SWD: three-pin header (SWCLK, GND, SWDIO) for a debug probe.
  • Not fitted from the reference: the ADC reference filter (no ADC use) and the on-board LED, replaced by the SBY "talking" LED on GP14 through 1 kΩ.

07Audio

  • MAX98357A (TQFN-16: DIN 1, GAIN_SLOT 2, SD_MODE 4, VDD 7–8, OUTP 9, OUTN 10, LRCLK 14, BCLK 16, GND 3/11/15 and the pad) on 5 V. GAIN_SLOT left open for 9 dB. SD_MODE from GP19 through 1 kΩ: high selects the left channel and enables the amplifier, low is shutdown, so the firmware mutes it while the chip is in standby and the speaker is silent between utterances. Speaker on a JST-PH 2-pin header; 8 Ω recommended (see power). 10 µF plus 100 nF at the VDD pins, 220 µF bulk on the 5 V rail near the amp.
  • Sample rate. The datasheet allows LRCLK only at 8, 16, 32, 44.1, 48, 88.2 or 96 kHz; 10 kHz is not among them. The I2S back end therefore runs the frame clock at 32 kHz and interpolates the chip's 10 kHz samples (ratio 3.2, fixed-point linear interpolation, a few cycles per sample). The PWM line-out keeps its native rate. Removing BCLK puts the part in standby on its own; the firmware also drops SD_MODE when idle.
  • Line-out kept from the 2023 design: GP11 PWM → 3.3 kΩ / 22 nF → 3.3 kΩ / 22 nF → 10 µF → 3.5 mm jack, with a 100 kΩ bleed to ground. Corner at 2.2 kHz. Solder jumper JP8 substitutes 33 kΩ for the second resistor for the original 33k "vintage" tone. No volume pot: level is set in firmware.
  • The firmware mirrors the same sample stream to both outputs; the I2S back end is a second `audio.c` implementation selected by the board define.

The ZX81's 7805 has little headroom, and Rigter's LM386 board drew of the order of 100–150 mA. Rev B stays in that range with an 8 Ω speaker and the firmware volume at its default. For a louder 4 Ω speaker, power the card from USB, where the amplifier's peaks are no longer the ZX81's problem. The 2068's supply is comfortable either way.

12Netlist

The whole board is written out pin by pin in hardware/revb/zxvoice-revb.net (Protel format, for EasyEDA Pro's File → Import → Netlist) with a flat CSV of every connection and a BOM CSV beside it, all generated by tools/revb_netlist.py. The generator refuses to emit anything unless every pin of every part is on exactly one net or explicitly declared no-connect, and every net has at least two members: 83 parts, 106 nets, 374 connections. Pinouts were taken from the datasheets (RP2040 QFN-56, W25Q32, ABM8, AP2112K, LVC245, LVC1G125, CD74HCT688 SCHS196E, HCT138, MAX98357A 19-6552). The line-out second stage is 3.3 kΩ + 30 kΩ with JP8 bridged across the 30 kΩ, which is how the "vintage" option is implemented without a second footprint.

13Layout notes

  • Decoder and comparator sit right behind the fingers; the three '245s and the '1G125 form the boundary between the 5 V half and the 3.3 V half, so no 5 V trace crosses into the RP2040 area.
  • RP2040, flash and crystal in the reference-design arrangement: flash within 10 mm of the QSPI pins, crystal and its capacitors on the same side with a ground pour beneath, USB pair routed as a 90 Ω differential pair with the 27 Ω resistors near the chip.
  • Amplifier and its 220 µF near the speaker header at the rear edge, away from the crystal. Speaker traces at least 0.5 mm wide.
  • USB-C, speaker header, line-out jack and the SBY LED all on the rear edge so they are reachable with the card plugged in.
  • Card outline: fingers on the front edge, rear edge about 65 mm back, width dictated by the 23-position connector (58.4 mm). Two layers are enough.

14Open items

  • Confirm the 2068 rear-connector key slot lines up with the TS1000 card key at position 3 so the card cannot be inserted three positions off, and that the 2068's +15 V at 3B is clear of the card's edge.
  • Check the 2068's A0–A12 drive: they come from the Z80 unbuffered (the schematic buffers only A13–A15 and the control lines). NMOS highs are well above 1.4 V, so HCT is safe; noted for completeness.
  • Decide whether the SWD header and the line-out jack are worth their board space on a production card; both can be DNP.
  • Firmware: a Rev B board define (pin map above, READY polarity, I2S back end, board ID on GP28/29) goes in before the first Rev B card is powered.
